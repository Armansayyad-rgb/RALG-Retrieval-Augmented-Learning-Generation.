Battery Monitoring Unit Operation Manual
========================================

1. SAFETY PRECAUTIONS
---------------------
- Always observe polarity when connecting or disconnecting battery terminals.
- Remove watches, rings, and metal jewelry before working on battery systems.
- Use insulated tools rated for electrical work.
- Work in well-ventilated areas - hydrogen gas may be present during charging.
- Never short battery terminals - can cause sparks, heat, or explosion.
- Wear acid-resistant gloves and eye protection when handling lead-acid batteries.
- Verify charging system is disconnected before performing maintenance.

2. NORMAL OPERATION
--------------------
- Battery voltage (fully charged): 12.6-12.8V (12V system) or 25.2-25.6V (24V system)
- Charge voltage: 14.4-14.8V (12V system) or 28.8-29.6V (24V system)
- Discharge cutoff: 10.5V (12V) or 21.0V (24V)
- Charge current: Limited by charger specification (typically 10-20% of Ah capacity)
- Operating temperature range: -20°C to 50°C (performance degrades above 30°C)
- State-of-health (SOH) threshold for replacement: <80% of rated capacity

3. STARTUP SEQUENCE
--------------------
a. Visually inspect battery for damage, cracks, or leakage.
b. Verify battery is correctly polarized (positive/+ and negative/-).
c. Confirm terminal connections are tight and free of corrosion.
d. Check electrolyte levels (flooded types only) - add distilled water if low.
e. Connect charger or load as appropriate.
f. Monitor voltage and current during initial charge/discharge.
g. Verify all monitoring readings are within normal range.

4. INSPECTION PHASE
--------------------
- Check battery case for cracks, swelling, or distortion.
- Inspect terminal connections for corrosion, looseness, or heat damage.
- Clean terminals with baking soda solution if corrosion present.
- Measure open-circuit voltage of each cell/unit.
- Measure specific gravity of electrolyte (flooded types only) - cells should be within 0.020 of each other.
- Inspect cables and connections for wear, overheating, or damage.
- Verify battery monitoring system readings match measured values.
- Check for abnormal heat during or after charge/discharge cycle.
- Inspect vent caps for proper function (flooded types only).

5. TROUBLESHOOTING
------------------

PROBLEM: Low voltage reading
- Cause: Battery discharged - recharge fully and re-test.
- Cause: Poor terminal connection - clean and tighten terminals.
- Cause: Cell failure - individual cell voltage lower than others.
- Cause: Battery beyond service life - replace battery.
- Cause: Parasitic load drawing power - identify and disconnect.

PROBLEM: Will not hold charge
- Cause: Battery sulfation - attempt equalization charge (flooded types only).
- Cause: Internal short cell - replace battery.
- Cause: Charger not functioning - verify charger output, replace if needed.
- Cause: Continuous parasitic load - identify and disconnect.

PROBLEM: Overheating during charge
- Cause: Charger voltage too high - adjust charger settings.
- Cause: Battery cell short - replace battery.
- Cause: Poor ventilation - improve airflow around battery.
- Cause: High ambient temperature - reduce charge current, improve cooling.

PROBLEM: High self-discharge
- Cause: Battery self-discharge beyond specification - may need replacement.
- Cause: Parasitic load from connected system - identify and disconnect.
- Cause: Internal cell degradation - replace battery.

PROBLEM: Monitoring system shows incorrect readings
- Cause: Sensor calibration drift - recalibrate monitoring system.
- Cause: Wiring fault - inspect connections, repair or replace wires.
- Cause: Monitoring unit firmware outdated - update firmware.

6. CHARGING PROCEDURE
-----------------------
- Connect charger to battery terminals (positive to positive, negative to negative).
- Set charger to appropriate voltage and current for battery type and size.
- Begin charging - monitor voltage and current.
- Charge until voltage reaches absorption level and current drops to trickle level.
- For flooded batteries: Equalize after full charge (controlled overcharge).
- Disconnect charger: negative first, then positive.
- Record voltage and charging time in maintenance log.

7. DISCHARGING PROCEDURE
-------------------------
- Connect load to battery terminals (respect polarity).
- Monitor voltage during discharge.
- Stop discharge when voltage reaches cutoff threshold (10.5V/21.0V).
- For deep-cycle applications: Do not discharge below 50% State of Charge for longest life.
- Disconnect load: negative first, then positive.

8. MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE
------------------------
- Daily: Check voltage readings, inspect for visible issues.
- Every 50 cycles: Equalize charge (flooded types only).
- Every 100 cycles: Clean terminals, check connection tightness.
- Every 200 cycles: Measure specific gravity (flooded types), check cell voltage balance.
- Every 500 cycles: Full capacity test, verify SOH.
- Annual: Comprehensive inspection, cleaning, testing per manufacturer spec.

9. EMERGENCY PROCEDURES
------------------------
- Acid spill: Neutralize with baking soda solution, flush with water, dispose per hazardous waste regulations.
- Battery fire: Use Class D fire extinguisher or abundant water - do not use CO2 or dry chemical.
- Severe acid exposure: Flush with water for 15 minutes, seek medical attention.
- Battery explosion hazard: Evacuate area, ventilate, do not restart.

10. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS
----------------------------
- Model: BMU-100 Battery Monitoring Unit
- System voltage: 12V or 24V configurable
- Capacity range: 50-1000 Ah
- Voltage measurement accuracy: ±0.1V
- Current measurement accuracy: ±2% of reading
- Temperature measurement: ±2°C
- Sampling rate: 1 reading per second
- Communication: Modbus RTU over RS-485, optional Bluetooth
- Display: 2-line LCD with real-time voltage, current, temperature, SOH
- Operating temperature: -20°C to 50°C (derating above 30°C)
- Protection class: IP65
- Connection: M8 circular connectors, screw terminals
- Alarm thresholds: Programmable, defaults at 20% SOH, voltage limits
- Certification: UL 1998, IEC 62933-1
